# The Count's end

## Overview
How do you kill the Count? The band is told the rules, and the novel
ends with his death, but not quite by those rules.

## Setup
- Chapter 18: Van Helsing tells the band how the Un-Dead are destroyed:
  a stake through the heart, then the head cut off. See
  [[rules-of-the-un-dead]].
- Chapter 23: the Count escapes from Piccadilly with a threat: "My
  revenge is just begun! I spread it over centuries, and time is on my
  side."

## Payoff
The sun is on the point of setting, and the Count's look is turning to
triumph, when Jonathan's Kukri cuts his throat and Morris's bowie knife
goes into his heart. The body crumbles to dust, the scar leaves Mina's
forehead, and the castle stands out against the red sky. See
[[death-of-the-count]].

## Open questions
<!-- wolfden: status=undecided -->
Is the Count really destroyed? There is no stake and no beheading as the
rules require: a knife to the throat, a knife to the heart, and dust. The
novel never settles it, and readers have argued both ways.

### Destroyed
<!-- wolfden: status=candidate -->
The knives count. The body turns to dust, the look of peace on his face
mirrors Lucy's after the stake, and Mina's scar fades at the moment he
dies, which only his true death could do.

### Escaped
<!-- wolfden: status=candidate -->
The sun had not quite set, so he still had power. He didn't die by stake
or beheading, and he dissolved into dust as he could into mist. The
"look of peace" is only what Mina wanted to see.
